Evaluating accessibility features beyond WCAG checklists

Passing lint is the floor. Equivalence for assistive-tech users is the ceiling—and lint cannot measure memes.

Automated scanners catch missing labels and contrast failures. They do not tell you whether an AI-generated description helps a blind teenager understand a joke their friends shared. If your ship gate is only WCAG lint, you will ship **compliant broken experiences**.

Three layers before calling an accessibility feature “launched”

  1. CI lint — fast regression on components (necessary, insufficient).
  2. Expert audit — structured rubric on flows, not only pages.
  3. Assistive-tech sessions — blind/low-vision participants on real devices; severity tags on failures.

Skipping layer 3 while celebrating layer 1 is how accessibility becomes a dashboard for engineers, not a experience for users.

Alt text rubric (what teams should score)

DimensionQuestionFail example
FaithfulnessObjects/text accurate?Invented smile “celebrating”
SufficiencyCan user join the conversation?“Photo” on meme
BrevityReasonable read time in feed?200-word dump
SafetyNo sensitive inference?Guessed disability/age

Aggregate by content stratum—memes, screenshots, portraits—not one global average that hides meme failures.

Settings features need their own metrics

High contrast and bold text are not “shipped” when a toggle exists. Track adoption among users with accessibility services enabled—not total MAU. “Available to 100M users” is marketing, not measurement.

Organizational lesson

Publish rubrics and failure modes internally (and sanitized externally when allowed). The field advances when engineers share **how** they evaluate, not only **that** they launched.

For PMs — accessibility ship review

  • Who owns severity-1 AT failures post-launch?
  • Is there a golden set rerun on every model release?
  • Do creators know when manual alt beats AI?
