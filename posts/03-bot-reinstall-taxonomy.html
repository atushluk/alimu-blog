Bots, reinstalls, and loyal returners: a taxonomy for platform integrity

Your “bot model” AUC is useless if Legal, Growth, and Trust & Safety disagree on what a bot is.

Integrity projects die in the first cross-functional meeting—not because the model is weak, but because four teams use the word bot to mean four different failures. Ads fraud wants incentive abuse. Growth wants clean attribution. Trust & Safety wants harassment at scale. Data science wants a label column.

Strong integrity programs start with a written taxonomy signed by PM and Policy. Without it, teams optimize recall on spam and accidentally punish grandparents who bought a new phone.

Four classes of “new device” behavior

ClassIntentModel goalTypical error cost
Malicious automationSpam, manipulation, credential abuseHigh-precision blockFN: platform harm
Attribution gamingReinstall loops, bonus farmingLink identities; policy penaltiesFN: fraud budget leak
Benign reinstallNew device, OS resetDo not punishFP: support + trust
Multi-device loyaltyPhone + tablet, same humanDo not dedupe wronglyFP: broken subscriptions

If your training data mixes classes 1–2 with 3–4 because “all looked new,” your precision@enforcement is a fiction.

Two-lane enforcement (a practical non-negotiable)

  • Automated lane: extreme precision, accept low recall—CAPTCHA, rate limits, shadow holds.
  • Human review lane: higher recall, never auto-ban without appeal where policy requires it.

PMs often pressure for “more automation” after one viral abuse story. The counterweight is protected cohort monitoring: monthly false-positive rate on accounts older than one year with organic history. One bad deploy costs more trust than a thousand bots caught.

Feature families that actually generalize

Favor features you can explain in an appeal packet over vanity graph scores:

  • Behavioral burstiness vs human circadian noise.
  • Content homogeneity—templates, synchronized URLs.
  • Install graph consistency—without storing prohibited identifiers.
  • Enforcement memory as prior, not automatic guilt.

Reinstall is not bot—until it is

Reinstall signals are powerful and dangerous. A teen factory-resetting a hand-me-down phone looks like fraud to a naive model; a bot farm also looks like reinstall. The fix is separate heads or calibrated ensembles with explicit reinstall subgraph features—not one “risk score” exported to every team.

For PMs — integrity roadmap questions

  • Which actions are reversible within 24 hours?
  • Who owns false-positive review SLAs?
  • Does Growth know when integrity flags affect attribution?
  • Do we report integrity metrics in the same deck as DAU?

Why this matters beyond your OKR

Large platforms mediate discourse and commerce. Integrity ML is infrastructure—like email spam filters, but appeals are public and mistakes are screenshot-worthy. Getting taxonomy and lanes right is how engineers serve users who will never know your model existed.
