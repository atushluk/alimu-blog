When to generate alt text: screen-reader triggers vs always-on inference

The launch metric is not “images described.” It is whether a blind user can participate in the same conversation as everyone else—without bankrupting your GPU budget.

If you are a PM shipping “AI accessibility” on a UGC feed, you will be asked for a single slide: we now describe every image. That slide is easy. It is also the wrong optimization target. Accessibility leaders, ML engineers, and counsel are optimizing different things—equivalence, cost, and liability—and a one-size pipeline makes all three unhappy.

This post is a production-minded view for anyone building on large consumer surfaces: how to architect alt text so it survives legal scrutiny, model upgrades, and the memes your vision model will confidently get wrong.

The metric most teams ship by mistake

Coverage (% of images with any alt string) is a vanity metric. Equivalence (can the user act on the post?) is the product metric. Confusing the two is how you earn a press cycle and still lose in a screen-reader session.

Sighted users infer context from layout, adjacent text, and culture. Screen-reader users get a linear audio channel. A lazy caption—“a person smiling”—can be worse than silence if it crowds out the comment thread or misstates a meme. Your job is not to maximize tokens; it is to minimize wrong tokens under time pressure.

Three architectures PMs actually choose among

ArchitectureWhen inference runsBest forFailure mode
Creator-only Never (manual alt only) High-trust communities, low photo volume Near-zero coverage at scale; fatigue
Always-on AI Upload or first view for all users Ads, regulated static content, search indexing Cost/latency; junk descriptions on decorative images
Assistive-tech triggered When AT focuses media and creator alt is empty High-volume UGC photo feeds First-focus delay; client signal privacy review

Most short-form products need a hybrid: creator text first, triggered AI second, human escalation third—not a global toggle chosen for the all-hands deck.

Why screen-reader-triggered generation deserves serious consideration

Deferring inference until VoiceOver, TalkBack, or equivalent accessibility services request description changes the unit economics. You spend GPU cycles where need is demonstrated, not hypothesized across billions of passive views.

  • Budget for quality: Lower QPS lets you run a heavier model or a second-pass refine on screenshots and dense text images.
  • Aligns with WCAG spirit: Equivalence is about users who rely on AT—not about invisible metadata for sighted users.
  • Reduces harm surface: Fewer unnecessary descriptions means fewer chances to hallucinate gender, race, or emotional state.
Non-negotiable: Triggered AI must never become an excuse to hide the creator alt field. Manual path stays prominent; AI is fallback, not default for creators who can type.

Tradeoffs worth flagging in every design review:

  • Cold-start latency on first focus—mitigate with async generation, accessible loading announcements, and aggressive caching by media_id.
  • Signal integrity: “AT active” must be defined with Privacy and Legal; do not fingerprint users beyond policy.
  • Decorative images: Explicit empty alt (alt="") still beats wrong AI; train PMs to ship creator UX for “decorative.”

When always-on still wins

Triggers are not a religion. Always-on at upload or first view remains correct when:

  • Moderation and accessibility share the same vision embedding—you already pay the inference tax.
  • Partner or regulatory commitments require description before publish (some ad and gov surfaces).
  • Search and discovery need text proxies for images regardless of AT.

The mistake is letting Marketing pick one story for all surfaces. Split pipelines by content class and attach different SLAs.

Evaluation that survives a staff review

Offline CIDEr on COCO will not save you when the 500th meme template arrives. A durable three-layer eval stack:

  1. Automated lint (missing alt, length caps, blocked terms)—CI gate on UI components.
  2. Human rubric on stratified samples: faithfulness, sufficiency, brevity, safety (1–5 each).
  3. Assistive-tech panel (small, paid, recurring)—severity-tagged failures, not NPS theater.

Report metrics with population and window: “U.S. photo posts, AT-triggered sessions, week of …”—never platform DAU as a proxy for your feature’s reach.

For PMs — questions before launch

  • What happens when creator alt exists but is low quality—override rules?
  • Who owns the error budget when memes regress after a model bump?
  • Is decorative content explicitly supported in creator tools?
  • Does Legal sign off on client AT signals and retention?

Takeaway

Accessible ML is scheduling, caching, creator UX, and evaluation—not only a checkpoint. The teams that win treat alt text as a system: manual first, conditional AI second, humans for severity-1 misses. That is how you ship equivalence at scale without treating blind users as an afterthought or a line item on a GPU invoice.
