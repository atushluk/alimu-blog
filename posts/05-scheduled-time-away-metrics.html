Scheduled time away: what product metrics can and cannot say about wellbeing

Feature retention is not sleep quality. Say what you measured—or say nothing.

Family pairing tools—scheduled quiet hours, curfews, meditation prompts—sit between parenting, adolescent autonomy, and corporate responsibility. PMs want compelling stats; researchers want causal health outcomes. Data scientists must translate without overclaiming, or you will lose credibility with policy teams exactly when you need them.

Split features, split hypotheses

Never bundle “wellbeing lift” across Time Away, curfew nudges, and reflective prompts in one KPI. Each feature has a different user story and a different defensible metric.

FeatureHypothesisDefensible metric
Scheduled Time AwayFamilies want enforceable off-hoursSchedule created & active at 7d/30d
Night curfew / meditationReduce late-night sessionsShift in session-start quantiles
Reflective promptsPause before endless scrollPrompt shown → user choice logged

The “98% kept it on” trap

Press-friendly retention numbers sound like health impact. They usually measure toggle persistence, not sleep, anxiety, or safety. Before any external claim, document:

  1. Denominator (which teens, which countries, which app version).
  2. Time window (first week vs 90 days).
  3. Definition of “retained” (never disabled vs used nightly).
Template sentence: “Among eligible Family Pairing teens in [cohort], [X]% did not disable [feature] within [N] days of activation.” Long, boring, defensible.

Predictive overuse models

Models that flag heavy session patterns should feed suggestions, not hidden penalties, unless Policy and Legal explicitly allow enforcement. Evaluate with precision on human-labeled sessions and track override rates—users dismissing nudges is signal, not failure.

State law alignment

Scheduled boundary tools increasingly connect to supervisory-tool statutes in various U.S. states. Analytics should prove feature availability and adoption in regulated cohorts—not claim anyone “solved teen mental health.”

For PMs — wellbeing reviews

  • Does this metric measure behavior or outcome?
  • Would a researcher accept the cohort definition?
  • Can teens or parents opt out without dark patterns?

Takeaway

Digital wellbeing earns trust through measurement humility. Families deserve tools and transparency—not headlines that confuse setting retention with health. That discipline is what separates serious safety product teams from checkbox PR.
